# First-Repository
https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/samartalwar/global-suicide-rates-and-socioeconomic-indicators/data

In this research I looked at national suicide rates around the world relate to national GDP to try and investigate how economic factors might play into suicidality. The data came from Samar Talwar who compiled the data from the WHO and World Bank. The data included different rates for each country for each year from 2000 to 2021. I aggregated the data by calculating mean suicide rates for each country over the twenty year period. The data also had separate rates for males and females which I aggregated as well.
The findings were that there was little to no correlation between suicide rates and a country's GDP or GDP per capita. And although male suicide rates were always higher than female suicide rates, GDP didn't seem to affect to affect them differently. Overall the research discounts the blanket statement that poorer countries or economies will have more suicides but further research is warranted to determine if other economic parameters such as relative inequality within a country or unemployment seem to have a noticeable association with suicide.
